Reference · business

How 3D Diagnostix Works

A dentist sends us a 3D X-ray of a patient's jaw. Days later a printed plastic template arrives that steers their drill to within a fraction of a millimetre of where a clinician planned it. Everything in this document is what has to happen in between — and what the software has to know to make it happen.

Who this is for

Someone new to 3DDX — an engineer, an analyst, or a model reading the codebase — who needs the business before the tables make sense. Read it top to bottom once. It builds: products, then people, then words, then one case end to end, then everything that makes a case different from that one.

1 · What we sell

3DDX sells guided implant surgery and the services around it. The centre of the business is the surgical guide: a printed template, custom to one patient, that constrains the surgeon's drill to a planned position. Everything else either feeds the guide, extends it, or is sold to the same doctors on the same account.

Two things follow from that, and they explain most of the system's design.

First, a case is not one product. A doctor who wants a guide usually also needs the scan converted, the model registered, the case planned, and often a crown to go on the implant afterwards. Each of those is separately priced, separately scheduled, separately staffed, and separately charged — but they share one patient, one set of files, and one clinical intent. So an order is a container, and the billable things live inside it.

Second, the work is partly physical. Design happens in Cairo; printing, inspection, packing and shipping happen in Boston. Stone models arrive by courier and have to be found again weeks later. A guide is not done when the file is done.

Read the counts carefully

Reports outnumber guides. Appliance/model work outnumbers treatment plans. The surgical guide is the strategic product and the hardest to build, but it is not the most frequent one — and a system tuned only for guides will be wrong about most of its own traffic.

2 · Who does what

Six operational groups touch a case. Knowing which one owns a case at a given moment is most of what the workflow pages exist to answer.

Customer Support

  • Owns the client relationship
  • Works from a task list, not a queue
  • Chases confirmations, models, surgery dates
  • Phone, tickets, live chat
  • Collections and account suspension

Treatment Planning

  • Planners plan and replicate
  • Fine-tuners run the doctor calls
  • Sleeve design and the instruction sheet
  • Its own QC team, after sleeve design
  • Coordinators handle CaseXchange and the calendar

Operations

  • 3D conversion
  • STL and dual-scan registration
  • Guide design
  • Assigns parts to printers
  • Works by locking one order at a time

Boston

  • Receives and archives packages
  • Scans and pours models
  • Prints, post-processes, sleeves
  • Final QC and photographs
  • Packs, labels and ships

Sales

  • Opens and owns accounts
  • Price lists and vouchers
  • Closes and commission
  • Follow-up rules on lapsing clients

Finance

  • Charging cycles
  • Invoices, payments, refunds
  • Extra charges after a price change
  • Radiologist payment sheets

Radiologists are a seventh group and sit slightly outside: they are contractors with their own queues, daily limits, state restrictions, vacation calendars and per-report fees, and they read reports rather than build guides.

3 · The vocabulary

This is the part that cannot be guessed. These terms appear in column names, status labels, email templates and conversation, and using them loosely produces wrong software. Definitions are from the Master Glossary and the departmental requirements.

Client
The account that sends us work. Almost always a dentist, oral surgeon or periodontist — but a scanning centre, a lab or a distributor can also hold an account. Account type changes what the account can see and be charged for.
Scanning centre
An imaging facility that takes the CBCT scan and submits the case on the doctor's behalf. It sees its own pricing, never the doctor's.
Patient
Never our customer, and never billed. In legacy a patient exists only as a name, date of birth and gender typed onto an order. In threeddx3 the patient is a pseudonymous patient_code; the name and date of birth sit in a separate, access-logged identity record.
Case
One patient, one clinical intent — above the orders. Usually one order; a double-arch request split into one order per arch is two orders under one case. Legacy has no case identifier (owner answer Q3); threeddx3 adds one.
Order
One submission from the client: one legacy Orders row. It belongs to exactly one case and carries one or more order items.
Order item / service line
One billable line: a 3D conversion, a treatment plan, a surgical guide, a report, a restoration. An order usually has several, and each runs its own workflow at its own speed. Legacy calls these sub-orders.
CBCT / DICOM
The 3D X-ray of the patient's jaw, and the file format it arrives in. Everything downstream depends on its quality.
STL
A surface mesh. Either an intra-oral scan or a scan of a stone model. Needed to know where the soft tissue and teeth surfaces are — the CBCT alone cannot show them.
3D conversion
Turning the raw DICOM into a planning file. Basic is a straight conversion; custom includes nerve mapping and segmentation. Priced per arch: quadrant or full arch.
Registration
Merging the STL onto the CBCT so the two coordinate systems line up. STL registration uses a model or intra-oral scan; dual scan uses a denture scanned separately with radiographic markers.
Treatment plan (TP)
Where the implants go. Produced by a planner in CoDiagnostiX. Planning is us deciding; replication is us re-drawing the doctor's own plan in our software; review and consultation are discussion sessions.
Fine-tuning (FT)
A scheduled video call where a 3DDX clinician walks the doctor through the plan and adjusts it live. 45 min for a quadrant, 60 for a full arch.
Fter
A fine-tuner: the clinician who runs those sessions.
Confirmation email
The moment the doctor approves the plan. Nothing is manufactured before it. Verbal approval during a fine-tuning session counts.
Sleeve design (S.D)
Choosing and placing the metal sleeves the drills pass through, and producing the instruction sheet. Done by the planning team, not operations.
Guide design
Building the physical body of the guide around those sleeves. Done by operations.
Surgical guide (SG)
The printed template that sits in the patient's mouth and steers the drill. Our flagship product.
Guide support
What the guide rests on: teeth, tissue or bone. It decides which registration must finish first — tooth-supported needs STL registration, tissue-supported needs dual scan, bone-supported needs neither.
Fitting surface (FS)
A printed replica of the anatomy, used to test that the guide seats without rocking before it ships.
PPG
Pre-positioning guide. Used when the guide has to be located before the osteotomy is cut. Forces the case through registration even when the support type would not otherwise require it.
Instruction sheet (IS)
The printed drilling protocol that ships with the guide.
Print plan (PP)
The build layout for the printer.
PSI
Pre-shipping instruction. A note that must be read and acted on in Boston before the box is closed.
Loaner kit
Our surgical drill kit, lent for one surgery and returned. Tracked by kit number, with an outbound and a return label created together.
Restoration
Crowns, abutments and bridges. Temporary or final — two separate lines, so one case can carry both.
Final AoX
Final full-arch restoration (renamed from Final Full Arch Restoration including temp). Thirteen solutions (Ti bar, Zirconia, PMMA, try-in …), each with its own status flow, parallel tracks and client approval gates.
FMP / GFMP
Full-mouth prosthetic design services; GFMP always bundles a treatment plan and a guide. They run last on a multi-service case, ahead of final restorations only. Whether each is a service, a form or both is still open (digest G16).
Relu
The AI implant-planning partner. A doctor may choose AI planning on a treatment plan; planners then review the AI's plan instead of planning by hand.
GFMR
Guided Full-Mouth Rehabilitation. The largest package we sell: treatment plan, guide and restoration bought together, with the voucher split across the three.
iSmile
The clear-aligner product line — aligners, retainers, night guards. Sold in tiers (Design, Esthetic, Smart, Unlimited), each capping aligner count, refinements and validity.
Voucher
Prepaid credit. The doctor pays up front for a block of work at a discount; each qualifying case draws it down. Every movement is a row in the voucher ledger.
Placeholder client
One “Unknown client” contact per tenant that owns legacy orders whose doctor account no longer exists (user decision U7), so no order is lost and none is attached to a guessed doctor.
Close
A commissionable sale, claimed by a sales rep. New client, activation, up-sell or follow-up — each has its own rules.
Option 1 / 2 / 3 / 4
The registration verdict. 1 = clean. 2 = clean but the doctor should be told something. 3 = usable with compromise, doctor must choose. 4 = unusable, we cannot proceed.
CaseXchange (CX)
CoDiagnostiX's own file exchange. Cases can arrive through it instead of through the portal, planned or unplanned.
Boston
The US site. Everything physical happens there: receiving, scanning models, printing, post-processing, QC, packing, shipping, archiving.
Archived package
The physical envelope a doctor sent — models, impressions, discs. Barcoded, linked to an order, stored in a bin, and pulled when the case ships.
Redo
A remake at our cost after a defect. Flagged so it is excluded from revenue and from radiologist payment.
DSO
Dental Support Organisation — a group practice buying on one price plan.

4 · The shape of a case

Six entities carry almost everything. If you understand these and how they nest, the rest of the schema is detail.

Account  (the doctor, the scanning centre, the lab)
  └── Case                    one patient, one clinical intent
        └── Order             one submission (two for a split double-arch request)
              ├── Service line      3D conversion          ─┐
              ├── Service line      treatment plan          │ each priced,
              ├── Service line      surgical guide          │ scheduled and
              └── Service line      restoration            ─┘ charged separately
                    ├── Child lines   iSmile stages, the services under a restoration
                    ├── Files         DICOM in, CAF/PDF/STL out (one file may serve several lines)
                    ├── Field values  the answers to that service's form
                    ├── Status history  every state it passed through
                    └── Charge        what was billed, and against which voucher

The case level is new in threeddx3. Legacy has no case identifier: when a doctor orders both arches, the portal splits the request into one order per arch and nothing records that the two belong together (owner answer Q3). Section 13 explains how the database now represents it.

Three details about that picture matter more than they look.

The service lines run independently. A conversion can be finished while the guide is still waiting for a model. Each has its own status, its own owner, its own due date. There is no single "order status" that means anything precise — which is why the workflow pages are per-service, not per-order.

Each service asks different questions. A guide order needs support type, kit brand, sleeve diameter, extra components. A report order needs date of birth, gender and which topics to evaluate. A restoration needs shade, material and retention. These are configured per service rather than hard-coded, which is why the answers live as field values rather than as columns.

Teeth are data. Missing teeth, teeth to extract, implant sites, abutment sites and crown sites are recorded per tooth, and drive both clinical work and price — a guide is priced per implant site, on a scale that runs from one site to sixteen.

5 · The journey of a guided case

This is the canonical path: a doctor wants a surgical guide, sends a CBCT, and gets a printed guide in the post. Read it once as the spine; section 6 is everything that deviates from it.

Client · portal or sender tool

The case arrives

The doctor uploads the CBCT and marks the teeth chart: what is missing, what will be extracted, where the implants go. They pick the services they want and any extras — biomodel, loaner kit, fixation pins, bone reduction guide.

If the account has default services configured, the missing ones are added automatically and a note is written to the order history saying so. If a service is unchecked on the price list, the portal does not offer it at all.

System

Intake checks

Rush is applied if the account always rushes. If the account is flagged as needing approval for this order type, the case is put on hold immediately and a CS task is raised. If the scans failed to upload, a task and an email go out the same day with a link straight back to the upload page.

Operations

3D conversion

An operator locks the conversion order, works it in CoDiagnostiX or Simplant, and uploads the result — a CAF file if the doctor owns the software, otherwise a PDF or screenshots. A checklist runs before it can be closed: patient name matches the scan and the file, file extension matches the client's configured format, nerve mapping done if the case is mandibular.

Boston · in parallel

The physical package arrives

Stone models or impressions come by courier. Boston scans the shipping label, links the package to an order, records the contents, scans or pours the models, uploads the STL, and files the package in a bin under a QR label. It stays there until the case ships.

Operations

Registration — and its verdict

The STL is registered onto the CBCT. The operator returns one of four options. Option 1 or 2 and the file is uploaded and the case moves on. Option 3 or 4 and the case stops: the operator picks findings from a fixed list, picks the options to offer the doctor, uploads screenshots, and an email goes out with a clickable link per option.

Treatment planning

Planning

A planner locks the case and plans the implants in CoDiagnostiX — or replicates a plan the doctor made in another package. Bad scans stop here: field of view cut off, low quality, old scan date, patient-name mismatch. Each reason sends its own email and raises its own CS task.

Fine-tuner + client

The conversation

Unless the account says otherwise, a session is booked and a clinician walks the doctor through the plan on a call, adjusting live. The doctor's approval is taken on the call or by the confirmation email that follows.

Client

Confirmation

The doctor approves. This is the gate: nothing physical is made before it. The confirmation records remarks — rush terms, disclaimers about scan quality, whether a flap or crestal incision is needed, implant engagement notes, grafting notes. Those remarks are carried forward into the after-confirmation email and onto the paperwork that ships with the guide.

Treatment planning

Sleeve design

Sleeves are chosen and placed, the instruction sheet is generated, and the after-confirmation email goes out — including a link for the doctor to set the surgery date if we do not have one.

Treatment planning · QC

Design review

A QC reviewer checks the case after sleeve design. They may update the documents, log a mistake against the designer, change the status, or send it back. Mistakes are classified major or minor and roll up into the designer's statistics.

Operations

Guide design

The guide body is built around the sleeves. The case only appears on this page once the right registration has closed for its support type. Another checklist runs: guide offset, guide span, undercuts eliminated, build file correct for the printer.

Operations · Boston

Printing

Parts are assigned to a printer and a build. Builds run early or overnight; overnight builds ship the next business day. A build report lists every part, and Boston prints the paperwork from it — shipping label, box label, print plan, instruction sheet, QC form. One shipping label per client, not per case, unless the practices differ.

Boston

Post-processing and final QC

Industrial prints get a steam bath, an oil bath and a wash; desktop prints get alcohol and a UV oven. Sleeves are fitted per the paperwork. Then the inspection: the guide is seated on the model or the fitting surface and must not rock, a drill is passed through each sleeve, the pre-shipping instruction is read and acted on, and the surgery date is checked against the shipping service chosen.

Boston

Ship

Photographs are taken of the label and of the guide on its model with the QC form behind it. The tracking number is attached, the order closes, and the archived package is released from its bin.

Finance

Charge

Closed and not yet charged makes the order eligible. When it is actually charged depends on the account: immediately, biweekly or monthly. Voucher credit is drawn down first; the remainder goes to the card, cheque or wire on file.

6 · Where it branches

Almost no case runs section 5 exactly. These are the deviations that the system has to support as first-class paths, not as exceptions.

Special requests

Each account carries standing instructions, grouped by the department that must obey them. When an operator or planner locks an order, the requests for their stage pop up and must be acknowledged one by one before the order opens. Changing a special request notifies the department and writes an entry on the account history naming the agent and the change.

Skipping the session

A standing request can say send the confirmation email directly, skipping the call, or switch to sleeve design directly, skipping both the call and the email. A per-case note overrides the standing request in either direction.

CaseXchange

The case arrives as a CoDiagnostiX file rather than through the portal. If it is already planned, it goes straight to confirmation or to a review session. If it is unplanned, it joins the planning queue. Coordinators archive the file against the order and read the doctor's annotations to decide which.

Replication instead of planning

The doctor planned it themselves — in Vatech, Planmeca, or with screenshots — and we redraw it in CoDiagnostiX. Without burnt-in implants or adequate screenshots the case is held and the doctor is asked to send screenshots or switch to a planning order.

Digital delivery

Some clients print in-house. The guide is designed and the STL is delivered instead of a printed part. Nothing goes to Boston, the status sequence ends at Done rather than Shipped, and the price is the digital one — the price book carries a digital and a manufacturing price for every option.

Registration option 3 or 4

The doctor is emailed a numbered list of findings and a numbered list of options, each a link. Whatever they click comes back as a CS task. The case cannot move until it is answered. Option 4 additionally means the file is never uploaded, so downstream stages never see the case at all.

Redo

Something was wrong and we remake it at our cost. The redo flag excludes the order from revenue reporting; for reports there is a separate switch that excludes it from the radiologist's payment sheet. A redo guide can skip sleeve design entirely and go straight back to guide design when only the model or support type changed.

Bone-supported guides

No registration is required — the guide rests on bone, so no soft-tissue surface is needed. The case goes to guide design as soon as sleeve design closes. Unless a PPG is involved, in which case registration is mandatory again.

GFMR

Treatment plan, guide and restoration sold as one package, with the prepaid credit split across the three by ratio and drawn down independently. It has its own status set, its own rejection state, and its own fabrication forms.

7 · The other product lines

Radiology report

The doctor uploads a CBCT and picks topics: rule-out pathology, implant planning, evaluate existing implants, sinus, TMJ, airway. TMJ needs open and closed bite scans. The report is auto-assigned to a radiologist — the account's own if it has one and they are available, otherwise anyone under their daily limit who reads for that state. Radiologists are paid per report by topic, monthly, from a sheet that excludes anything marked exclude from radiologist payment.

Model registration and laser scan

The physical intake path. A package arrives, Boston links it to an order, records what is inside — maxilla and mandible models, maxilla and mandible impressions, anything else — and marks each as used the same day or stored for later. Impressions poured in house add a fee to the order. Everything scanned that day appears on the Molds Received Report, which is saved so any department can answer "did it arrive?".

Restoration

Crowns and abutments, temporary or final. The order carries material, retention type, shade system and shade value, and can be per-site rather than uniform. Costs are itemised — abutment, crown, implant analog, impression, shipping, rush, arch, chairside assistance. A digital wax-up is often produced before a fine-tuning session so the doctor can see the end state while the implants are still being placed on screen.

iSmile aligners

A header order holds the patient and the arch; sub-orders hold the chargeable stages — model, planning, fabrication. The tier bought caps aligner count, refinements and validity, and the tier applied is recorded per order so later price changes do not rewrite history. Production is Boston: print the models, thermoform the sheets, trim, wash, label each tray n of N, and count sheets used so waste is measurable.

Software licences

CoDiagnostiX client and producer licences, renewals, upgrades and click packs. Each carries a dongle number, a licence number and an expiry. Boston verifies that the number on the box matches the number on the dongle before shipping.

Vouchers

A voucher is created as an order because it is charged like one. It names the account billed, the accounts allowed to consume it, an amount, an expiry, and either a credit value or a number of cases — and it can be restricted to one service type or left general. Installments are supported: the voucher leaves the automatic charging cycle and re-enters on the dates agreed.

8 · Money

Price is per client, per option, per currency

There is no single price list. Every client can have their own price for every option of every service, in USD, EUR or GBP, with separate digital and manufacturing prices. A guide is priced as a base plus a per-implant-site increment on a sixteen-step scale, plus each extra component, plus shipping and rush. That structure is why the legacy client table has 627 columns.

The voucher ledger

Credit is drawn down in a defined order — general first, then oldest first, then cases before additional credit before original credit. A deduction can be edited, in which case the original amount is refunded to the voucher and the new one taken. An order can be excluded from voucher use entirely, which refunds the credit and puts the amount back on the invoice.

Charging and invoicing

Charging is a daily cycle over everything closed and not yet charged, filtered by each account's setting. Card charges go through the payment gateway; cheques and wires are recorded when they land. Invoices are generated on the account's own schedule — per closed order, per shipped order, weekly, biweekly, monthly, on named dates, or not at all. A failed card is emailed to the client at most once a week, no matter how many times the cycle retries.

Closes and commission

A close is a commissionable sale, categorised as new client, activation, up-sell or follow-up, each with its own qualifying rules. Closes are added when the order arrives and move to their month when it is charged. The rules are specific enough to be worth encoding rather than judging: lead source must be set, vouchers must be at least half paid, only one close per batch for a brand-new client, consultation never counts.

9 · The physical side

Locking

Design work is done by locking an order. A locked order disappears from everyone else's list, and unlocks automatically when the file is uploaded. Unlocking by hand demands a reason and notifies management with how long the lock was held. Lock duration is the raw material for turnaround time, productivity and the investigation of anything that went wrong.

Checklists and quality

Every hand-off has a checklist, and the checklists are configurable rather than coded. Operations has one per stage; Boston has one per product; QC scores are weighted per section and per item, and deleting an item deprecates it rather than removing it so historical analysis stays comparable.

Batch records

Regulated products carry a full production record: when scans and models arrived, who poured, scanned and archived them, who converted, planned, designed and reviewed, which printer and which resin, the temperature and duration of every bath and the UV oven, which sleeve article went into which site, who inspected, and when it shipped. This is the traceability that lets a complaint be traced back to a specific build.

Inventory and loaner kits

Consumables are decremented as they are used or sold — resins, thermoforming sheets, drills, fixation pins, sleeves by article number. Loaner kits are tracked individually: available, out, or on hold pending a replacement part. Sending one creates both the outbound and the return label at once, and the return is chased from the day after surgery.

Archiving

Physical packages are barcoded on arrival, linked to an order, and stored in a numbered bin. They are pulled when the case ships. The link between an order and a physical shelf location is the only way to answer "where is the doctor's model?" — and that question is asked constantly.

10 · Customer service is a task engine

CS does not work a queue of cases; it works a list of tasks, grouped by account. Most tasks are raised by the system, not by a person, and each has a due date derived from what triggered it.

The same shape repeats elsewhere. Technical support runs tickets through open, in progress and closed. Collections runs a task list generated from unpaid invoices, with the power to suspend an account — which pulls its orders off every workflow page without deleting them, and puts them back on unsuspend.

Why this matters for the migration

These integers cannot be decoded without knowing which product line the row belongs to. Any mapping that treats status as a single global lookup will assign a plausible but wrong status to a large share of the order book — and row-count reconciliation will not catch it, because every row still migrates. The threeddx3 ETL gives every item the workflow definition of its own (order type, digital, service) coordinates — the legacy OrdersFlow table distinguishes eighteen service dimensions, which v2 collapsed (review finding F05) — and maps the legacy status id to a state of that definition through workflow.legacy_status_codes.

13 · How threeddx3 records it

Sections 1–12 describe the business. This section describes how the third and final PostgreSQL schema, threeddx3, represents the parts of it that changed since v2 — because the owners answered the open questions, because the review found defects, or because new requirements arrived (Final AoX, FMP, Relu AI planning, uploads, notifications). Tables link to the schema guide; value lists are quoted from the database's own column comments.

13.1 · Cases above orders

A case is the clinical container; orders hang under it. Every order belongs to exactly one case (orders.cases). When a doctor orders both arches the portal splits the request into one order per arch; those two orders should be one case. Legacy never recorded the link, so it has to be inferred from the same patient, doctor and scanning centre and a close creation time (owner answer Q3).

database comment · orders.cases

Clinical case above one or more orders (Q3: no legacy case id exists). One row per case: a single order is a one-order case; a split-arch submission (one order per arch) becomes two orders under one case.

The rule the owners set (U8): historical pairs are linked automatically only when the evidence is unambiguous; anything else stays as separate one-order cases and is listed for a person to decide in orders.case_pairing_candidates, with the evidence and the score kept. The migration today creates one single case per legacy order and writes every candidate pair as pending; nobody's history is merged by a guess.

A correction found while building v3

The per-service records under a restoration or an “other services” line (legacy SubOrderServices) point at the line, not at the order. v2 joined them to the order number and attached them to unrelated orders. v3 hangs each service under its own line. The consequence for the business: the count of “children whose parent order was deleted” reported in the v2 validation (1,394) came from that wrong join; with the correct join it is roughly 168. The owners' decision (Q21) still applies — true parentless children are excluded and listed in the exclusion ledger — but the number changes. The technical guide has the detail.

Two legacy line tables are retired by owner decision (Q21): training orders (OrderTrn) and the old ortho table (OrderOrtho). Their rows are not migrated; each is recorded in audit.migration_exclusions with its reason, so the reconciliation can prove nothing was lost silently.

13.3 · The voucher ledger

A voucher's balance is never typed in. Every movement — the purchase, each service it paid for, each refund, each correction — is a row in vouchers.voucher_transactions, and the balance on vouchers.vouchers is maintained from those rows by the database. A movement is never edited or deleted; a correction is a new row.

Legacy recorded consumption late and partly, so the owners named voucher_fill.php — the script 3DDX already uses to rebuild a voucher's history — as the authoritative rule (Q19). threeddx3 runs the same rule, deterministically, for every voucher:

Opening

The purchase

One issue row for the voucher's cost, dated when it was bought.

Consumption

Each service it paid for

One redeem row per consuming service, with the amount recorded on that service and the time the service completed. Which services count depends on what the voucher was for (conversion, report, plan, guide, general, restorations, GFMR, iSmile).

Manual edits

What staff changed by hand

Deductions and refunds made on the edit-voucher page are kept as recorded.

Reconciliation

Match the balance the client sees

If the rebuilt history does not end at the balance the application shows today, one adjustment row closes the gap — a deduction for missing consumption, a credit for extra. Every voucher therefore ends exactly at its current balance, and the adjustment says how far the legacy record was off.

13.5 · Final AoX and FMP / GFMP

Final AoX (thirteen full-arch solutions) and the FMP / GFMP services introduced three ideas the earlier schema did not have.

  • Parallel tracks. One item can run several flows at once — try-in, Ti bar and Zirconia — each a workflow.workflow_instances row with its own track and state.
  • Approval gates and client decisions. Some states stop until the doctor approves. The doctor answers through a single-use emailed link (orders.confirmation_links, only the hash of the token is stored) or in Connect, and every answer is an append-only row in orders.order_item_decisions — a regulated design-approval record.
  • Holds, zero-price lines, assignment and sequencing. A hold is either the customer's or internal; a line priced at zero must say why; who is assigned to an item has a history (orders.order_item_assignments); and the order in which a case's items may progress (FMP last, Ti bar before Zirconia, GFMR before GFMP) is recorded in orders.order_item_dependencies.

13.8 · Clients and staff

What the client record now holds that it did not:

  • Door types — the legacy bitmask is decoded into five yes/no facts on crm.contacts: registered, CBCT door, sales door, contract door, scan-centre lead.
  • Tooth numbering the client works in (storage is always FDI) and the best time to call; pilot users of new features.
  • Typed phones (crm.contact_phones), the equipment a client owns (crm.contact_equipment), competitors they also use (crm.contact_competitors), feedback (crm.feedback) and sales targets (crm.sales_targets).
  • Special requests by department, with who may see each category (crm.special_request_categories).
  • The single placeholder client per tenant for orders whose doctor account no longer exists (U7).

And for staff:

  • No legacy password is carried over (U3). Legacy passwords were reversibly encrypted; every migrated user must set a new password through a reset at first sign-in, and recent passwords cannot be re-used (iam.password_history).
  • Sales pairs — the 21 opener/closer pairs — are an internal staff relationship (iam.staff_relationships); what a pair means in practice is still to be confirmed by Sales (Q20).
  • Working hours per person or team (iam.work_schedules), used with the team's business calendar for assignment and SLAs.

13.9 · Patients and privacy

The patient record everyone works with (clinical.patients) is a code, the owning client and the legacy key — no name, no date of birth. The identity sits in clinical.patient_identities, which no application or reporting login can read directly: the application asks for it through a function that requires a stated purpose and writes an access-log row each time, and reporting users see only initials and birth year. Dates of birth that legacy stored encrypted are decoded during the migration; values that cannot be decoded are quarantined for manual resolution rather than guessed. The technical guide and the compliance checklist describe the controls.
